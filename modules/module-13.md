The assignment includes **30 questions** divided into three sections:

- **Section A (1–10): Conceptual Understanding.** Ethical AI, algorithmic bias, IEEE Ethically Aligned Design, UNESCO, OECD, and the EU AI Act.
- **Section B (11–20): Applied and Scenario-Based.** Hiring discrimination, autonomous vehicles, predictive policing, human-in-the-loop decision-making, privacy, deepfakes, surveillance, finance, healthcare, and representative datasets.
- **Section C (21–30): Reflective and Future-Oriented.** Bias, governance, quantum AI, intellectual property, sustainability, emerging ethics careers, 2035 risks, and my professional responsibilities.

## What I learned

One of the most important ideas in this module is **human-in-the-loop oversight**. I believe AI should support people rather than replace human responsibility, especially when decisions affect someone's education, employment, health, or safety. Even a technically accurate system needs meaningful review, opportunities to correct errors, and clear accountability.

This connects to earlier work in telecommunications, where I used AI and simulations to analyze network traffic, resource allocation, and performance. Ethical development means evaluating not just how well a system performs but also how it affects the people using it.
