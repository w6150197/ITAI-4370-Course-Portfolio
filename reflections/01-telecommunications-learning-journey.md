# Reflection 1 — How My Understanding of Telecommunications Evolved

At the start of ITAI 4370, I thought about telecommunications mainly as the transfer of information between devices. The early assignments made me pay attention to the components that make that possible: transmitters, receivers, channels, modulation, bandwidth, noise, and latency. Simulating BPSK was particularly useful because I could see the signal change instead of only reading a definition.

The next modules moved from individual signals to entire networks. Comparing 4G and 5G architecture helped me understand why network functions, slicing, and edge computing affect service quality. The path-loss simulation showed me that a wireless link has physical limitations, while Wireshark showed how actual communication can be inspected at the packet level.

Later, I began thinking of networks as systems that must be measured, managed, and adapted. I studied Open RAN, resource allocation, security, and energy efficiency. By Module 11, I could connect these earlier ideas to proposed 6G capabilities, AI-native architecture, distributed intelligence, and digital twin networks.

My biggest improvement was learning to connect theory with practical evidence. I still want more experience with real network hardware and production traffic, because classroom simulations simplify many conditions. Overall, the course helped me move from recognizing network terminology to interpreting how network components and algorithms work together.
